Topics of Interest

Three tracks, one theme.

Submissions may target any of the three tracks. Interdisciplinary work is especially welcome; papers that cross legal, technical, and empirical methods will be given priority in the plenary review.

Track A

Agentic AI & Consent

The mechanics and ethics of delegation:

  • Meaningful consent for agent-mediated action
  • Human-in-the-loop design patterns
  • Multi-agent negotiation and privacy
  • Preference learning and user autonomy
Track B

Cross-border Data

Interoperability across jurisdictions:

  • APEC CBPR / EU GDPR convergence
  • Korea's PIPA in agentic contexts
  • Data localization vs agent portability
  • Case studies of cross-border enforcement
Track C

Audit & Assurance

Proving what a system did, and why:

  • Accountability records & provenance
  • Red-teaming methodologies for agents
  • Third-party audit standards
  • Post-incident forensics
Submission Guidelines

What to submit, and how.

Format

Papers must be submitted in English, formatted with a 12-point serif body face, single-column, 1.5 line spacing. Abstracts: up to 500 words. Full papers: up to 8 pages including references. Anonymized for double-blind review.

Review

Each submission receives at least two peer reviews. The program committee is composed of scholars and practitioners from partner institutions across the Asia-Pacific. Reviews focus on originality, methodological rigor, and relevance to the 2026 theme.

Presentation

Accepted authors present a 15-minute talk in the afternoon parallel sessions, followed by a 5-minute discussion. Selected papers will be invited to a special issue of a partner journal.

Ethics

Submissions involving human subjects or sensitive data must include a statement of ethical review. The Forum requires that all work involving AI systems adhere to transparent reporting on training data provenance and model behavior.

Ready to Submit

The portal opens March 2026.
